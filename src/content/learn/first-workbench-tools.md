After this page you can pick out a small starter toolkit, choose the right glue, and build your first plastic kit at a kitchen table. Most mistakes are easy to fix, so start small and learn as you go.

Buy nothing before your first club visit; the club has shared tools at the layout. If you volunteer, email first through [Contact](/contact).

## First project: clean up one part

Try this with your first kit before you buy anything else:

1. Find a part on the **sprue** (the plastic frame that kit parts come attached to).
2. Snip it free with flush cutters, a little away from the part.
3. Trim the leftover nub flush with a hobby knife, using a few light passes.
4. Glue it to its neighbor with plastic cement, hold it for a few seconds, and let it dry.

Rules for this step:

- Wear safety glasses. Snipped bits fly farther than you expect.
- Cut away from your body, and keep your other hand behind the blade.

## Starter tools

| Tool | What it does | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hobby knife | Trims parts and cuts thin strips | A slim handle that takes standard #11 blades, such as X-Acto or Excel |
| Cutting mat | Protects the table | Self-healing, at least letter-paper size |
| Flush cutters | Snip parts off the sprue | One flat cutting face and fine jaws, such as Xuron |
| Fine-point tweezers | Hold tiny parts | Tips that meet evenly when you squeeze |
| Steel ruler | Measures and guides straight cuts | A cork or rubber back and clear marks |
| Plastic cement and CA | Join parts | Thin liquid cement for plastic, and super glue with a fine tip |
| Desk lamp | Shows edges, seams, and glue | A bright LED on an arm you can aim |
| Safety glasses | Protect your eyes | A Z87 marking on the frame |

**Rough total:** about $75 to $160, or about $55 to $120 if you already own a good lamp. Prices are rough and change over time.

That covers many simple plastic kits. Read the kit instructions first, since some kits also call for files, small screwdrivers, or a drill. For wood kits, add wood glue.

**Optional upgrades later:** a pin vise (small hand drill) with tiny drill bits, needle files and sanding sticks, jeweler's screwdrivers, and a headband magnifier.

## Knife and cutter habits

- **Replace a dull blade.** A dull blade needs more force, so it slips more often.
- **Make several light passes** instead of one hard cut.
- **Let a falling knife drop.** Step back instead of grabbing for it.
- **Keep old blades in a lidded container,** such as a pill bottle, and tape it shut before it goes in the trash.
- **Use sprue cutters on plastic only.** Cut wire with cutters made for wire.

## Which glue for which job

Find the two materials you are joining, then test on a scrap piece first.

| Joining | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Plastic to plastic | Plastic cement. It softens both surfaces so they fuse. Tamiya Extra Thin is a common pick. |
| Metal, resin, or mixed materials | CA (super glue). Thin CA sets in seconds. Thicker "gap-filling" CA gives you time to line parts up. |
| Wood, paper, card, scenery | White or wood glue (PVA). It dries nearly clear. |
| Heavy parts | Two-part epoxy. It is stronger and less brittle than CA. |
| Clear windows | Glue labeled safe for clear parts |

Plastic cement does not bond metal, wood, or slippery plastics such as nylon. Use CA, epoxy, a pin, or a screw for those.

Keep CA away from:

- **Clear parts.** Its fumes leave a white haze.
- **Cotton cloth and cotton balls.** CA can heat up fast in cotton and burn you. Work over a tray or card.
- **Moving parts.** A drop in a gear or wheel can freeze it solid.

A CA debonder lets you undo a joint, and an accelerator spray makes CA set faster. Both are useful but optional.

## Set up your bench

1. Cover the table with your cutting mat.
2. Work over a light-colored tray with a raised edge, so small parts stay put and are easy to spot.
3. Leave parts on the sprue until you need them.
4. Keep fresh air moving, and follow the label on every glue.
5. Cap glues and blades, and keep them away from children and pets.

## If glue or a blade slips

- **Glued fingers:** do not pull them apart. Soak them in warm, soapy water and gently roll the skin apart.
- **Glue in the eye:** rinse with water and get medical help.
- **Small cut:** press with a clean cloth until it stops, rinse, and cover it.
